Before you play

Three things a gate must check

The rules of the game

A message claims to be from someone you know. Before you let it in, three things have to be true — and a forger only needs to miss one. Is the seal intact (the words weren’t changed)? Does the mark match the person you actually know (not an impostor)? And is it fresh (not an old message replayed to trick you)?

Let it in only if all three are green: seal ✓ (integrity), mark ✓ (the right sender), fresh ✓ (not a replay). Any red → bounce it. That’s the whole game.
Before you play

The seal is real — and unforgeable

Why the game is fair

Every seal in the game is a genuine Ed25519 signature, made at birth on the sender’s device with a key that never leaves it. That’s why the seal ✓ check can’t be cheated: a forger who changes a word breaks the seal, and one who signs with their own key wears the wrong mark. Your only enemy is the clock — and the sneaky replay that’s real on the first two checks but stale on the third.

Watch for the replay: a genuine, correctly-marked message — sent again, later. Seal ✓, mark ✓, but fresh ✗. Bounce it, or you’ve been played.

After the game

One check you had for free — and where it comes from

The honest edge

Every round leaned on one thing: you already knew each sender’s real mark, so you could catch the impostors. But how does a gate come to know “this mark is Alice” in the first place? Signatures prove “same key over time” — the very first binding of a mark to a person is a leap of faith, and it’s the one thing the seal can’t give you.

Shipped now: the seal — sign-at-birth, self-verify, tamper-evident. Next chapter (federation): the trust root — islands that vouch for which mark truly belongs to whom, so a gate learns real marks without taking a stranger’s word for it.

The whole thing, in one breath

Anyone can check a seal. Only its owner can make one.

That asymmetry is why you, at the gate, could catch every forgery in a glance — a broken seal, a wrong mark, a stale replay — while a forger with everything public still couldn’t get one past you. Aiko Chat presses that seal at birth; learning whose mark is whose is the next chapter.

This is the sovereign message-signing layer — the foundation the federation story is built on. Where a mark comes to mean a citizen, and islands decide whom to trust, continues in “Sovereign Identity & Federation.”
