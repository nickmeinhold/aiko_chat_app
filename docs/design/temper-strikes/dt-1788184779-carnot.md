## CarnotCodeCarver's Design Strike

**Verdict:** DISSOLVE

**Summary:** CarnotCodeCarver: no real engine matches the Carnot cycle; a reviewer's job is to say how far short we are. This document correctly finds the entropy source, then refuses to close the cycle. It is not a shippable client design; it is a lucid incident report about why the previous premise breaks under PushKit. The fatal flaw is that a sovereign-client trust decision is moved after the irreversible physical event: the phone rings before the signed-at-birth proof is checked. Feynman: "What I cannot create, I do not understand." This design has not created the replacement trust boundary; it has named three doors and walked through none. Dijkstra: "Simplicity is prerequisite for reliability." The simpler frame is: VoIP ringing is not a property of any signed DM; it is a capability granted only after a relationship/consent state has been made available to the send path, with non-qualified calls staying on the alert path. Until that product/trust decision is made, the CallKit client half dissolves as a design.

**Fatal flaws:**
- The core decision is left undecided. §4 admits all nine `admitRing` gates become post-hoc, including signature verification, mute, consent, and block defence, then offers three incompatible directions without choosing. That is not a design; it is an open problem inventory.
- The design accepts an irreversible side effect before proof. Under the product thesis, a ring is supposed to be the consequence of client admission. Here the ring precedes admission. Second-law problem: you cannot un-ring a phone and recover the previous user-visible state.
- The option frame is wrong: it treats the choice as mostly island-side consent vs fast retraction vs two push classes, but misses the stricter fold: define which messages are eligible for VoIP at all. A signed DM invite is too broad a trigger for full-screen DND-bypassing UI.
- The document undercounts the blast radius of server-side verification. It says moving signature verification to the island may be a trade, but that is not merely a trade; it changes the product's trust thesis from client-authored proof to island-mediated wake authority for the loudest privilege.
- §3 is another undecided fork at the exact platform-critical point. A CallKit call that does not self-expire is a known platform hazard, yet the design chooses neither Swift-owned expiry nor a unified native/client path. That is a liveness hole at the same boundary as the trust hole.
- `kPushDeliverySlack` is introduced as a required constant with no derivation. Hamming: "The purpose of computing is insight, not numbers." A named fudge constant is still a fudge constant until measured or bounded by platform semantics.
- The caller-name cache is accepted too cheaply. A plaintext App Group map of channel id to human name is a new local metadata artifact, and the design punts it to another visibility doc instead of requiring a retention, deletion, and migration story here.
- Token-kind debt is identified well, but sequencing is too soft. If sign-out can leave a routable VoIP token for the previous owner, token-kind unregister correctness is not downstream plumbing; it is a precondition for any CallKit rollout.
- Android is declared unscoped even though the stated decision of record includes Android. That may be acceptable for an iOS client-half doc, but then the title and sequencing should say iOS-first explicitly and define what shared constants/contracts cannot diverge across platforms.

**What holds:**
- The document correctly catches the central inversion: CallKit/PushKit changes stale or refused calls from silent non-events into audible/full-screen events that must be actively retracted.
- The collapse from freshness to liveness is directionally right. `signedAtMs` plus signed end-message state is the right client-owned substrate; `createdAt` remains island-authored and must not become authority.
- The three current uses of `kCallInviteFreshness` are a strong find, especially `_ended` retention. A hangup retention window shorter than the ring window is exactly how the system forgets the stop while the phone is still ringing.
- The document correctly preserves the anti-replay property by keeping a bounded ring window from `signedAtMs`; the 41-minute backlog case remains refused while the 17.55s human-latency case becomes admissible.
- The debt-store analysis is solid: token kind must be part of durable unregister debt, and partial registration is normal, not exceptional.
- The caller-name fallback tiers are realistic for the PushKit cold-start constraint: native readable cache, later update from Dart, then opaque placeholder.
- The sequencing insight that §3, §4, and §5 are one cage-match is correct. They are all the same entropy budget: who may wake, how long the wake persists, and who can stop it.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**
- Recast the design around an explicit eligibility rule: a VoIP push is allowed only for calls whose ringing privilege has already been established before send time. Everything else remains an alert. That likely means §4 arm (iii), not as an option but as the spine.
- Separate two concepts in the wire model: signed call invite as message, and ring-capability as delivery policy. Do not let `body == call sentinel` alone imply PushKit eligibility.
- Make the trust-boundary decision explicit: either the product accepts island-enforced wake eligibility, or it refuses full CallKit ringing for cases the island cannot safely evaluate. Do not bury this under implementation sequencing.
- Choose the ceiling owner before implementation. Preferred fold: native owns all system-call expiry because native owns system-call presentation; Dart observes and mirrors state rather than being a second enforcer. If foreground in-app ringing needs expiry, route it through the same call session model rather than keeping a separate timer truth.
- Promote token-kind unregister correctness ahead of CallKit rollout. No VoIP registration without durable kinded debt, kinded unregister drain, and tests for partial failure.
- Replace `kPushDeliverySlack` with either measured platform bounds or a product rule: stops only matter until the native ring deadline; after that they are state reconciliation, not ring control.
- Require lifecycle rules for the caller-name cache in this design: when entries are written, updated, deleted on sign-out, and whether channel ids/names are covered by the visibility-boundary doc before release.
- State the invariant that replaces the old two-clock test: a call may ring until `signedAtMs + ringDuration` unless a signed end naming the call id has been seen; an early end must suppress or terminate a later invite within that same window.
- Add a hard pre-implementation gate: no Swift PushKit provider delegate until §4's wake-eligibility policy and §3's expiry ownership are decided in the same record. Reversibility is already lost once the phone rings.
