## KelvinBitBrawler's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** The design correctly identifies a trust-boundary-violating cold fault but freezes before selecting the one path that resolves it, leaving the product's core thesis exposed to absolute zero.

**Fatal flaws:**
- **§4 presents a false choice that abdicates its duty.** The design's central, chilling finding is that an unverified, potentially forged, invite can ring a handset through Do Not Disturb. It then frames the fix as a choice between A) violating the product's core thesis by trusting the island, B) shipping a broken-by-design user experience, or C) a third path. This is not a choice to be delegated. A design that finds a fatal flaw must strike it, not document it as an open question. `Roy Batty: "I've seen things you people wouldn't believe... All those moments will be lost in time, like tears in rain."` This flaw cannot be lost in a list of options. The design's failure is one of nerve.
- **§4's proposed solution is presented as an option, not the answer.** Arm (iii), "Two push classes," is the only path that resolves the trust boundary violation without inverting the product's security model. By making the privileged VoIP push a property of an *established* relationship, it bounds the "ring before proof" problem so it no longer applies to arbitrary, un-vetted strangers. This is the correct thermodynamic path, moving the system to a lower energy state. By presenting it as one of three equivalent "product calls," the design fails to recognize it as the engineering solution to its own primary finding.
- **§1 introduces an undefined constant, `kPushDeliverySlack`, as a structural component.** This is a hand-wave where a physical model is required. A retention policy for a hangup message that depends on a magic number is how you get a phone ringing for a corpse. This isn't a value to be tuned; it's a duration that must be *derived* from the physical constraints of the delivery system it's meant to outlast. Leaving it as a "fudge" is a thermodynamic impossibility; entropy will find the gap.

**What holds:**
- The analysis of the "two clocks" problem in §1 is a crystalline piece of forensics. The design correctly identified that a single constant was load-bearing in three different, entangled ways.
- The pivot from "freshness" to "liveness" in §2 is correct and moves the model closer to the truth.
- The diligence on downstream consequences is solid: the analysis of the ring ceiling (§3), the `PendingUnregisterStore` debt (§5), and the plaintext caller-name cache exposure (§6) are all valuable and correct. This design is an excellent map of the blast radius.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**
- **§4 must be rewritten to select arm (iii) as the definitive path.** It is not a "product call" among equals; it is the only engineering choice that resolves the trust boundary flaw without compromising the system's thesis. The design must state that a VoIP push is a privilege reserved for contacts who have already passed the trust gates on the quieter `alert` path. First contact is an alert; an established relationship earns a ring.
- **§1's `kPushDeliverySlack` must be replaced with a derived value.** The design must propose a model for it. For example: "the 99th-percentile delivery time for a high-priority push to a sleeping device, plus the island's own retry-and-expire window for a hangup message." This makes it a concrete, physical constant that can be measured and argued, not a guess.
- **The open question on signature ordering must be struck.** With §4 arm (iii) adopted, the question is answered: unverified callers do not get a VoIP push. The ring *still* happens before the signature check, but only for callers who have already been vetted. The risk is bounded, not eliminated, and the design must state this new, residual risk plainly.
