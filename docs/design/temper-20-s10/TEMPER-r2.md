# TEMPER round 2 — design 20 §10 v3.1

**Overall verdict:** RECAST (4/4 families seated, none DISSOLVE). Round 2 of ≤3.
**Struck:** dt-1792892702, 2026-10-25. Maxwell + Noether + Carnot + Tesla; no dark seat.

## Per-family verdicts

## Fatal flaws (deduped, most severe first)
1. **A stolen device can't be revoked.** All four raised it. Under ADR-0009 ref. 4 the lost phone holds the Ed25519 key, so it signs epoch n+1 itself, and it can sign a signing-key change too. No in-band rotation authenticates the legitimate owner. **DISPOSITION: Nick** (this re-prices ADR-0009 Q1, decided this morning on an under-priced cost), and the fold says plainly that recovery needs out-of-band re-verification (#236, §9.4.1's QR).
2. **Rollback by omission and a cold cache.** Raised by Noether, Carnot and Tesla. The island can serve the old statement and never the new one, a fresh install has no high-water mark, and the cached K at ring time can be dead. **DISPOSITION: fold.** Every signed message carries the sender's current K epoch inside its signature, so freshness is observable through speech, which the island can't forge or rewrite. A caller seals only to a K whose epoch matches the latest seen on a message. The bytes go to the specialist.
3. **The deploy ordering contradicts itself.** Raised by Carnot and Tesla. **DISPOSITION: fold.** Suppression is per device, by capability: a device that can open sealed rings declares it when it registers its push token. The island stops unsealed VoIP **only toward those devices**. Old apps keep today's wake and tier-3 ring, the island can deploy first without silencing anyone, and the island's task #21 changes shape.
4. **The wake has no rate.** Raised by Noether, Tesla and Carnot. The island's shape check can't tell a real seal from junk, so any peer who has spoken can force report-then-end. **DISPOSITION: fold, owned by the island.** The ring ceiling becomes a contracted per-(caller, callee) rate, not a citation. Plus a hardware measurement owed (Maxwell): what a locked iPhone shows and sounds during report-then-end.
5. **The native store isn't a design.** Raised by Noether, Carnot, Tesla and Maxwell. **DISPOSITION: fold.** Split secret custody (the private K in the Keychain, `AfterFirstUnlock`, with that cost named) from policy data (a versioned snapshot replaced atomically, keyed per account, never "the active account"). The admission requirements live inside the signed and sealed invite: freshness, intended recipient, conversation id and the caller's name, so the island can't choose the consent bucket or relabel the call.
6. **Groups are declared covered, not designed.** Raised by Carnot and Tesla. **DISPOSITION: fold, by descoping.** §10 v1 is DM-only. Channel rings need their own fan-out design; until then they don't seal, and under §10.5 they don't system-ring.
7. **Smaller items.** Pins are directional: the callee must have pinned the caller too (Noether). "Human-sent" is unenforceable; the pin comes from any signed message (Maxwell). §10.6.3 is stale (Tesla). The crypto queue still describes the per-DM carrier (Carnot). **DISPOSITION: fold.**

**Refuted:** Noether says report-then-end resetting the must-report counter is "asserted, not measured". It was measured: #4178/#4278, recorded at `AppDelegate.swift:476` ("`reportNewIncomingCall` then immediately `reportCall(endedAt:)` COUNTS as reported"). The bundle didn't include it. What's unmeasured is what the user *sees*, and that's Maxwell's flaw in 4.

## Disposition
Fold 2–7. 1 goes to Nick. Round 3, the last, after the fold.

## MaxwellMergeSlam's Design Strike (round 2)

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** The round-1 fold holds on carrier and gate 7. But §10.1's anti-fork claim is false while the thief holds the copied signing key, and §10.3 and §10.5 both rest on two platform facts nobody has measured.

`Ellen Ripley: "Did IQs just drop sharply while I was away?"` Round 1's folds are honest. The new text added three claims nobody checked.

**Fatal flaws:**
- **§10.1 "a lost phone can't fork the pin" is false (broken invariant).** ADR-0009 ref. 4 copies **both** keys to every device. A thief holding the lost phone holds the Ed25519 key, so it can sign an epoch n+1 statement with a K of its own choosing, and peers will accept it, because it's higher and validly signed. An X25519-only rotation therefore revokes nothing. **Recovering from a lost device must rotate the signing key**, and a signing-key change is what #236 surfaces. §10.1 must say that, and drop the claim that the epoch alone protects the pin.
- **§10.3's native view needs the private K readable on a locked phone, and that weakens its protection (unstated assumption).** PushKit wakes a locked handset, so the private K must sit in a Keychain class readable while locked (`AfterFirstUnlock`), weaker than `WhenUnlocked`. Before the first unlock after a reboot, nothing is readable, so §10.3's "unreadable means not admitted" silences every ring until the user unlocks once. Both are acceptable, but they're costs §10 doesn't name. They also tie to crypto queue item 9: no Keychain class is pinned for the signing key today.
- **§10.5's whole "no ring" promise rests on report-then-end being invisible, and that's unmeasured (missing failure mode).** #4278 measured that report-then-end *counts* as a report. Nobody has measured what a locked phone *shows or sounds* during it. If it flashes the lock screen or chirps, then a peer who has spoken but isn't allowed can flash the callee's screen at 3am at the island's ring-ceiling rate. That's exactly the attention harm consent exists for. It needs a hardware measurement before the design relies on it.
- **The "human-sent" pin is unenforceable as written (overclaim).** §10.0 step 2 says the pin comes from "a real message that person sent". Cryptographically it's any message signed by that key. A resident agent, or a client sending automated messages, pins just as well. The honest wording is "pinned from a signed message in the conversation". The protection is that the island can't forge one, not that a human typed it.

**What holds:**
- The account-level statement in the IdentityDoc, accepted only for a pinned signing key, folds round-1 flaws 2, 3 and 7 honestly, and the island has confirmed gate 7 holds.
- Nick's ruling is implemented correctly in two halves (no wake request without K; unsealed push always report-then-end), and the release ordering rule is explicit.
- §10.3 withdraws the false "unchanged", and a missing view means "not admitted".
- §10.6.4 records what the island is owed, including that presence of the field alone isn't enough.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**
- §10.1: "a lost device is revoked by rotating the **signing** key (and K with it); an X25519-only rotation is a routine refresh, not a revocation". Delete the claim that the lower epoch protects the pin.
- §10.3: name the Keychain class the private K needs (`AfterFirstUnlock`), the cost (weaker at rest), and the silence before the first unlock after a reboot. Link to queue item 9.
- §10.5: add a hardware measurement owed before build: what a locked iPhone shows and sounds during report-then-end. If it's visible, report-then-end is not "no ring", and the ring ceiling becomes the only bound on lock-screen flashes.
- §10.0 step 2: "pinned from a signed message in that conversation", not "human-sent".


## NoetherNullPointer's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** The conserved quantity §10 v3.1 quietly stops conserving is *the rotation epoch is a monotone counter with a single authority* — and §10 hands the pen to two authorities it does not name: the lost device (which holds the shared signing key, so it can mint ahead) and the island (which is the sole server, so it can serve behind). "It can't fork the pin" is false on both paths.

**Fatal flaws:**

- **§10.1's rotation rule is a dishonest fold of round-1 flaw 4 (broken invariant: "only the legitimate device advances the epoch").** Tesla's round-1 strike said the lost phone "holds K and can announce it again, signed by the same key the legitimate phone is signing with." The fold answered: *"Its old statements carry a lower epoch, so it can't fork the pin."* That answer only holds if the lost device lacks the signing key. **ADR-0009 ref. 4 gives every device the same Ed25519 key.** So the lost phone does not replay epoch *n* — it signs epoch *n+1*, and the "reject a superseded statement" rule (which is aimed at the *island* replaying old bytes) does not touch it. §10.1's own escape — "a change of the signing key is a key change #236 must surface" — is unavailable for an X25519-only rotation, and even a signing-key change must be signed by the *old* key, which the lost phone also holds. Until an out-of-band, human-confirmed key change happens, a stolen handset is a full forking authority over every peer that still trusts the pin. The round-1 point was named, not folded.

- **§10.1's rollback defence assumes the observer has seen the latest statement, but the island is the only server (broken invariant: monotonicity is observable).** "A peer accepts only a higher epoch and rejects a superseded statement" bites only when a superseded statement is *present beside* a newer one. A caller with an empty cache — or any caller re-fetching — gets whatever the island serves from `verificationMethod[]`. The island can serve the **old** statement and never the new one; the caller has no higher epoch to compare against and accepts the stale K. **Omission-rollback is not replay-rollback**, and §10.1 names only the latter. The design treats the island as a *replayer* and misses that it is also the *omitting authority*.

- **§10.5's wake bound is asserted, not enforced (unstated assumption: the caller is compliant; missing failure mode).** §10.6.4's island gate is a **shape** check (§8.1) on a caller-supplied field — deliberately content-blind. So the island's "sealed" predicate is *present + valid shape*, while the callee's ring predicate is *opens + verifies + consented*. The gap between those two predicates is exactly the report-then-end flash, and any peer who passes gate 7 can mint a valid-shape blob that seals to nothing and wake the handset **every time**. §10.5 names "the island's ring ceiling" as the bound but never defines it; the island tab's own answer says it "has no stake beyond gate 7 limiting how often it happens" — but gate 7 is a **boolean** (*has spoken*), not a rate. There is no conserved per-peer wake budget; there is a boolean and a flash.

- **§10.5(c)'s linchpin — "report-then-end counts as a report and resets the counter" — is asserted, not measured (load-bearing unverified claim).** #4278 measured the *four-unreported-push* termination; the bundle shows no measurement that a report immediately followed by `reportCall(endedAt:)` resets the counter, nor that a rapid report+end is not itself throttled. If it is not a report for counter purposes, (c) degrades to *"the hostile island terminates the app,"* which is the exact failure (c) exists to avoid. The whole product decision rests on this one un-cited behaviour.

- **§10.3 puts the device's private X25519 key in the same native-readable view as consent and pins, and calls the view "untrusted until verified" (coupling to remove; category error).** A private key is a secret, not a claim; *verify* has no meaning for it. The view must be readable by native before Dart runs — which is precisely the store class **queue item 9** flags as unpinned (secure-storage accessibility/backup options). §10.3 never says which store the private K lives in, only that the view "holds" it. Secret custody and policy data are two jobs; they should not share a store whose other contents are "untrusted until verified." And §10.3's ordering claim — *"a revocation lands before the next push can read stale consent"* — is stated as a requirement with no mechanism (no lock, no version counter, no atomic swap); the concurrent-arrival path (revocation in flight, push lands mid-write) is exactly where it fails, and the design leaves it undefined.

- **§10.0.2's "can be sealed to and has spoken coincide" is directional, and a ring needs both directions (broken invariant: symmetry).** A's pin of B (from B's speech anywhere, §10.4) lets A seal to B; it says nothing about B's pin of A. A can hold B's K — pinned from a channel — and seal a ring while B has never pinned A, so B's verify fails → report-then-end, and A hears a ring that never rings. §10.6.3 names only the reverse edge (no K to seal to); the one-directional case is left to the flash.

**What holds:**

- **Cutting consent from key distribution is sound and survives.** Consent never touches the wire; withdrawal sends nothing; the island learns neither who allowed whom nor who withdrew. §9's leak is genuinely closed.
- **The IdentityDoc socket is the right home for the statement.** One account-level document, served opaque, forged by no one (the island holds no signing key). It is the socket ADR-0009 already opened, and building it once for both the statement and the link is correct.
- **Pin-from-speech, and the statement leaving the DM, honestly fold round-1 flaw 2.** Gate 7 counts only real messages again; the design does not hand itself the gate. §10.4's correction (sealability may run *ahead* of wakeability, never behind) is the right note.
- **§10.2's rules hold:** own inner domain tag, signed separately from the carrier; signer structure in the tag, not in the signature count; one layout for statement and link. That matches the existing `msg:v1`/`react:v1` discipline.
- **§8.1/§8.2 belong to the sealed push envelope, not the statement**, and the island's refusal to shape-check the statement is right for the reason given.
- **Nick's §10.5 direction is a decided product constraint, and the strike is on its implementation, not the preference.** Caller-side restraint and callee-side report-then-end are the right *shapes*; what is missing is the bound, the mechanism, and the measurement behind them.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**

- **§10.1 — stop claiming the epoch converges on its own.** State plainly that with one copied key, an X25519-only rotation is **indistinguishable from a lost device** by epoch alone, and that any rotation (X25519 or signing) must surface as a #236 key change requiring out-of-band confirmation before the old K is retired. State that until then the lost device is a full forking authority, so nobody plans a lost phone as "just rotate." And name the island as an **omitting** authority, not only a replaying one: a peer must retain the highest epoch it has *ever seen* and reject any served document that does not carry it, rather than trusting the served set.
- **§10.5 — define the bound or delete the claim.** Either name the "island's ring ceiling" concretely (mechanism and units) or replace it with a per-peer wake budget the island actually enforces, since gate 7 is a boolean and cannot be a rate. Separately, cite the measurement that report-then-end resets the CallKit counter, or mark (c)'s linchpin unverified — it is the whole decision.
- **§10.3 — separate the secret from the policy.** Name the store class for the private K (the platform secure store, per queue item 9) and say the view holds a *reference* to it, not the secret; drop "untrusted until verified" for the key. Then specify the read/write mechanism — a version counter or an atomic swap — that makes *"a revocation lands before the next push reads stale consent"* true under a push that arrives mid-write.
- **§10.0.2 / §10.6.3 — make the pin two-directional, and name the one-way case.** A ring requires *both* the caller's cached K *and* the callee's pin of the caller. Say what the caller sees when the callee has not pinned it (today: the silent flash), and decide whether that edge is acceptable or gated.

## CarnotCodeCarver's Design Strike (round 2)

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** The largest source of wasted work is removed: account metadata no longer travels as chat speech. Nick's refusal ruling is folded honestly. But rotation, native admission and group delivery remain promises rather than architectural decisions. Dijkstra: "Simplicity is prerequisite for reliability." No real engine matches the Carnot cycle; a reviewer's job is to say how far short we are. Here, the entropy hides in state ownership and recovery.

**Fatal flaws:**
- §10.1 mistakes a monotone counter for compromised-device recovery. ADR-0009 gives the lost phone the signing key too. It can sign a higher epoch; an account-level document does not prevent that fork. Even without compromise, an offline or restored peer that never observed n+1 cannot identify signed n as superseded. Round-1 flaw 4 is only partially folded: rollback protection after observation is supplied, but recovery, concurrent writers and suppressed successors remain unresolved. 'It can't fork the pin' is false under the stated copied-key model.
- §10.3 names a native-readable view without establishing an authoritative owner. 'The push handler owns it' and 'Dart writes it' leave the consent commit boundary unspecified. Saying revocation must land before the next push merely restates the desired outcome. Interrupted writes, an already-running admission, account switches and restored stale snapshots still have no ordering semantics. Nor is private-key availability while locked established; queue item 9 explicitly says accessibility is unpinned. Round-1 flaw 5 was acknowledged, not completed.
- The admission table still omits the evidence connecting an authenticated signer to this particular authorised call. Signature verification alone does not establish freshness, intended recipient, conversation scope or trusted display name. The allowlist header explicitly says sender.userId and sender.label are outside the signature. A hostile island must not be able to transplant an allowed signer's invite into another conversation, replay it as a new call, or attach a new name. Round 1 explicitly requested trusted presentation data; §10.3 dropped it. The specialist owns the construction, but the architecture must first state these admission requirements.
- §10.4 folds group coverage by declaration. One account key removes per-conversation key publication; it does not make a group invite deliverable to multiple recipients with different account keys. The design specifies neither the delivery unit nor who produces each recipient's sealed material, how missing recipient keys affect other recipients, or where the APNs budget applies. Round-1 flaw 7 remains partly open. 'Channels get it for free' conceals the remaining fan-out and partial-delivery decisions.
- The release boundary is not executable as written. §10.5 removes tier 3 in the same app release that adds sealing, while §10.6 deploys the island's no-blob suppression first. That island change immediately affects installed old callers, before the replacement app is available or adopted. Nick accepted compatibility loss; the defect is claiming a same-release ordering safeguard that cannot coordinate independently deployed islands and clients. Separately, the specialist queue still describes per-DM publication, per-DM pins and the old carrier. The blocking review would receive the architecture this round supposedly retired.

**What holds:**
- Round-1 flaws 2 and 3 are honestly folded: removing automatic DM posts preserves gate 7 without message classification, unread churn or repeated publication. The correction that IdentityDoc requires new island storage, endpoints and a wire contract is explicit.
- Round-1 flaws 1 and 6 are honestly resolved by Nick's ruling: no cached key means no caller-requested VoIP wake; refused or unverifiable pushes take report-then-end. The accepted presentation cost is stated. This review does not reopen that preference.
- Consent remains device-local, per account and conversation, keyed on signing material. Publication and withdrawal reveal no explicit consent event.
- Separate signing and encryption keys, purpose-specific attestations, envelope bounds and specialist ownership remain sound constraints. A statement authenticates K relative to an established signing-key pin; it does not independently authenticate an account or person.
- Account-wide key publication covers key discovery across conversations. Sealability preceding per-DM wakeability is harmless while the island independently enforces gate 7.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**
- Replace the lost-phone convergence claim with explicit recovery semantics. Distinguish encryption-key rotation from replacement of a compromised signing authority, define concurrent-device and restored-peer behaviour, and state what suppression can leave unresolved. Epochs protect remembered history; they do not manufacture a trusted latest state.
- Give native admission one authoritative state owner and a defined commit point. Specify atomic snapshots, account isolation, revocation versus in-flight admission, crash recovery and locked-device key availability. Remove duplicated mutable authority wherever possible.
- Complete the admission contract with freshness, duplicate-call handling, recipient/account and conversation binding, and a locally trusted presentation source. Missing evidence takes the already-decided refusal path. Leave encodings and cryptographic mechanisms to the specialist.
- Define group delivery and partial failure before calling groups covered. Keep account key discovery shared; explicitly count the recipient-specific delivery work and its payload bounds.
- Write the deployment transition for old and new callers, recipients and islands, including when compatibility loss begins. Update the cryptographer's queue to §10 v3.1 before requesting sign-off. Sharing a carrier with future links should not make building the entire link feature a prerequisite.

## Tesla, the Arc-Prophet's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** §10 v3.1 honestly lifts the key off the DM and writes Nick's branch down, then leaves the wake, the epoch, and the lock-screen read ticking on clocks the cache and the adversary still own.

**Fatal flaws:**

- **The caller-side half of §10.5 is a hymn the wire cannot sing (§10.5, §10.6, against §0 and §8.1).** "No cached K, no VoIP wake" is enforced by the honest app. The island's only test is §8.1's shape check, and §8.1's whole virtue is that the island cannot tell a seal from padding. A gate-7 peer stuffs a version byte and a length and buys the same wake. Task #21 therefore stops empty fields, and it does not stop the wake. Against the hostile island §0 still names, gate 7, the ring ceiling, and `block` are the adversary's own metronome: every push must be reported or the fourth unreported one kills delivery (#4278), so report-then-end fires at APNs' rate. The ceiling is cited and not contracted, which is §8.2's third clock left in someone else's head. The slogan "doesn't get the lock screen" and the mechanism "CallKit shows the call and ends it" are the same sentence. Round-1 flaw 1 is folded as a choice of branch (c), and its bound is still credited to the party the branch cannot bind. Round-1 flaw 6 killed the long unverified ring. The flash that remains is unbounded exactly when the threat model is live.

- **The epoch rule and the ring-time rule cancel each other (§10.0.3, §10.1, §10.5's "never behind").** A superseded statement is rejected only by a peer who has already seen a higher one. §10.0.3 forbids the fetch at the moment of sealing, so the caller seals to the cached K. After a rotation that cache is the dead key: the stolen handset opens the invite, the new one never sees it, and there is no unsealed fallback because fallback was correctly killed. That dark window is the steady state after every recovery ADR-0009 allows, and §10.5 prices only the adoption tail. The lost phone is not a stack of old statements. It holds the same Ed25519 (ADR-0009 ref. 4), so it is a live writer of epoch n+1. A high epoch signed by the key the user still calls current poisons the high-water mark. "Reject superseded" then rejects the legitimate successor. Nothing says a user-confirmed signing-key change clears that mark, and nothing surfaces an X25519-only change under a stable signing key, so the thief's rotation is silent where #236 would have spoken. A fresh install has no mark at all. The island serves it the dead epoch and the monotone check blesses it. The island's "sealability never runs behind wakeability" is copied into §10.5 and is false whenever the cache is cold: you have spoken, gate 7 would wake, the caller has no fresh K and, by §10.5, does not ask. Round-1 flaw 4 folded the document and missed the cache, the second writer, and the empty memory.

- **§10.4 answers the channel question with the wrong predicate.** Account-level K does cover channels. Consent defaulting OFF does not decide whether a channel ring seals, and it does not decide whether the island wakes. Three clocks: have K, gate 7 in that channel, consent on this device. The fold names the first and cites the third as if it were the second. The resident agent the allowlist header was recut for has spoken in every channel it sits in. Under branch (c) that is one report-then-end flash per channel, per push, and the membership set is the gain. Round-1 flaw 7 is folded for the key and left open for the sleeper.

- **The admission view states an invariant it has no mechanism for (§10.3, outcome table in §10.5).** "A revocation lands before the next push" is not a thing iOS can promise. An in-flight VoIP push reads a present, stale view. The safe state they specified is missing. The dangerous state is readable and old, and that state produces a verified, named ring after the user withdrew. The private K has to be readable on a locked handset, which is the moment file protection is tightest. No protection class is named, so the likely defaults fail closed into report-then-end for every consented call after lock, or they leave the secret in a store the push path can open. The view lists account, consent, pins, and K, and it does not list the name the outcome table shows. A verified key plus an island-supplied label is the relabel attack the allowlist header exists to stop. The lookup key is also unnamed: if consent is selected by payload `c`, the island picks the bucket. §4b moved the call id inside the seal for this class of trick, and the consent index was left outside. "The active account", singular, plus clear-on-switch, drops every background account on the floor. ADR-0009's whole premise is two accounts on one handset. Round-1 flaw 5 withdrew "unchanged" and replaced it with a law of ordering. The store is still not a design.

- **The deploy clock strikes at T+0 of the island, not at the end of the tail (§10.5 sequencing, §10.6).** Every ring today is unsealed. "Stop VoIP when the sealed field is absent" plus "island deploys first" means the installed base sends no blob, the island sends no wake, and every locked phone goes quiet for the whole app-store gap. §10.5 forbids removing tier 3 before the sealed path exists, then §10.6 removes it on the server first. "Cut to match the app version" and "deploys first" are two sentences about one cutover. §10.6.3 is stale in the same breath: the island's correction, sealability running ahead of DM speech, is already in §10.5, while the open item still says a connected callee who hasn't spoken has no key.

**What holds:**

- Round-1 flaw 2 is an honest fold. The statement left the DM. Nothing automatic satisfies `_spoken_here`. The island agreed on 2026-10-25, and the pin is taken from a message a person sent. The badge is not minted by the announcement.
- Round-1 flaw 3 is an honest fold, including the cost. One account statement in `verificationMethod[]`, fetched when a conversation opens. The island's correction is in the text: no IdentityDoc exists today (`mentions.py:21`), so this is storage, an endpoint, a wire shape, and a cage-match, built once for this statement and the link.
- The product half of flaws 1 and 6 is honest. The trilemma is written, (c) is chosen by name, tier 3 is dead as a full ring, and the callee table is the right artifact. Withdrawal sends nothing. Consent never becomes an island record. §9 stays buried. I am not striking that preference.
- Decision 6 still holds for Apple: opaque blob plus `c`. Two keys, the specialist's ownership of bytes, the statement's own domain tag, and the signer structure living in the tag. A missing or unreadable view fails closed. That last rule is the one the rest of §10.3 should have been built from.
- The monotone document is the right shape. What fails is trusting it at ring time from a cache, and trusting every copy of the signing key as one writer.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**

- In §10.5, rewrite half 1 as an honest-client optimization. State that a shape-valid field is not a seal, that any gate-7 peer can force report-then-end, and that a hostile island's rate is APNs because the ceiling is the island's own. Keep branch (c). Delete "doesn't get the lock screen" and write the event that actually happens: CallKit presents and ends, the screen wakes, no name is shown. Either put the ring ceiling's number and its scope into this contract or stop citing it. Add the header reword here, so "how the wake presents" is the doctrine, not a chore left to a later edit.
- In §10.0.3 and §10.1, split the clocks. A caller may seal only to a cached statement whose epoch was refreshed inside a bound the design names. A cold or stale cache takes the in-app path and must not seal to the previous K. State the rotation cost beside the adoption cost: rings stay dark until that refresh, and the refresh is not the ring gesture. State the acceptance rule the specialist will encode: any device holding the signing key can publish the next epoch; an X25519 change under a stable signing key is surfaced, not applied in silence; a user-confirmed signing-key change supersedes every epoch of the old key; a client with no high-water mark must not treat the island's blob as newer than one the user has confirmed. Strike "never behind" and write the cold-cache case, where wakeability is ahead of sealability.
- In §10.4, write the three predicates on separate lines. Consent defaulting OFF selects report-then-end after the wake. It does not keep the island from sending. Name the agent's blast radius as one flash per channel where that agent has spoken.
- In §10.3, replace "lands before the next push" with the race you can actually build. The handler reads one generation. A view that is stale, half-written, or for another account is the missing view: report-then-end. Name the file-protection class that is readable when the handset is locked, because that is the only moment this path runs. Put the CallKit name in the view as a local label bound to the pinned key, and say the payload's label is never that name. Say the consent row is taken from the identity inside the opened seal. Say how a push for an account that is not the foreground one finds its own view, or admit that background accounts do not ring.
- In §10.6, make one cutover sentence. The island keeps waking unsealed invites from app versions that have not shipped sealing. It applies "no blob, no wake" only to the version that seals and has dropped tier 3. Retire the open item that says a connected, not-yet-spoken callee has no key. Point §10.6.2 at the queue and say items 1 and 4 still describe a per-DM post. The specialist must be briefed on this section, or the dissolved carrier comes back wearing a domain tag.
